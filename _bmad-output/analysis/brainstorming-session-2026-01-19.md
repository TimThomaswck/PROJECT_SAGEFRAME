# Brainstorming Session Results

**Facilitator:** Mary
**Date:** 2026-01-19

## Session Overview

**Topic:** A personal assistant-style productivity tool with a wide range of integrated features.

**Goals:** To explore ideas for core features, information management, focus/time management, and a comprehensive list of other potential features.

### Feature Brain-dump

Here is the initial list of desired features:

- **Fast capture inbox** – quick add for thoughts, tasks, ideas.
- **Smart task manager** – due dates, priorities, context tags, natural language entry.
- **Hierarchical tasks** – subtasks & checklists that don’t hide behind clicks.
- **Recurring task logic** – flexible patterns that don’t reset your progress.
- **Projects with goals** – each project knows its outcome and next steps.
- **Dependencies** – tasks can block/unblock each other.
- **Kanban/Vision board & list views** – board mode or simple lists.
- **Calendar sync** – bi-directional with Google/Calender, not just read-only.
- **Time blocking** – schedule tasks into your calendar (Notion/Calendar apps).
- **Energy/effort estimation** – estimate effort, not just deadline.
- **Intelligent scheduling** – game-plans your day based on capacity.
- **Task suggestions** – gentle nudges for overdue or neglected items.
- **Offline access** – everything works even without network.
- **Notes with backlinks** – networked notes like in Obsidian/API integration to obsidian.
- **Rich text/Markdown editor** – supports code blocks, tables, embeds.
- **Spaced repetition memory** – Anki-style review baked in for key notes.
- **Idea linking & knowledge graph** – see connections rather than files.
- **Daily, weekly, monthly reviews** – guided prompts (like journal check-ins).
- **Habit tracker** – non-shaming streaks & habit chains (Streaks/Loop habits).
- **Focus modes** – block distractions, reduce UI noise (Forest/Focus@Will vibes).
- **Pomodoro support** – built-in timers with customizable intervals.
- **Attachment support** – images, files, links, and integration with cloud drives.
- **Smart templates** – reusable workflows (Notion/ClickUp templates).
- **Tags + smart filters** – combine contexts flexibly.
- **Pan-workspace search** – fast, fuzzy find across tasks, notes, calendar.
- **Version history & undo** – rollback states like in Google Docs/Notion.
- **Comments & threads** – in tasks and notes.
- **Notifications hub** – digest mode so you don’t get spammed.
- **Dark/light themes** – comfort for late-night work.
- **Exportable data** – plain-text/Markdown so nothing’s trapped.
- **APIs + integrations** – email, calendar, file services.
- **Privacy-first sync** – encrypted where possible, so you own your data.
- **Progress analytics** – streaks, focus time, trends (Optional dashboards).
- **API/automation support** – Zapier/IFTTT style hooks for power users.
- **Smart insights** – subtle suggestions like “you often postpone these.”
- **Goal planning & outcomes** – break big goals into measurable results.
- **Resource linking** – link references to tasks (videos, article highlights).
- **Journal & reflection space** – quick mood + accomplishment logs.

## Technique Selection

**Approach:** AI-Recommended Techniques
**Analysis Context:** A personal assistant-style productivity tool with a focus on exploring ideas for core features, information management, focus/time management, and a comprehensive list of other potential features.

**Recommended Techniques:**

- **What If Scenarios:** This technique was recommended to break through conventional thinking and explore radical possibilities for the productivity tool's features, helping to imagine truly novel functionalities.
- **SCAMPER Method:** Following the broad ideation of "What If," this technique provides a systematic framework to refine, expand, and innovate on existing and new feature concepts.

**AI Rationale:** This two-phase approach is designed to first maximize creative and unconventional thinking, and then channel that creativity into a structured process, ensuring a comprehensive and well-developed set of feature ideas.

## Technique Execution Results

Our brainstorming session was a dynamic and collaborative exploration, resulting in 13 distinct, high-quality ideas that define the core of the personal assistant.

### Creative Facilitation Narrative

We began with a divergent "What If Scenarios" technique, which allowed us to generate a wide range of foundational concepts, from the assistant's core personality to its ability to digitize physical notes. The user's active participation and clear feedback were crucial in shaping these initial ideas. We then transitioned to the SCAMPER method, a convergent technique. This phase was highly effective, as the user skillfully combined, adapted, and modified our initial concepts, leading to sophisticated, integrated features like the "Quest Log Dashboard" and the "Hub-and-Spoke Knowledge Workflow." The journey from broad concepts to refined, actionable ideas demonstrates a powerful creative partnership.

### Generated and Refined Ideas

**[Idea #1]**: The Jarvis Co-Pilot (Enhanced Communication)
*   _Concept_: An AI assistant with a calm, gentle, but firm personality. Its primary communication is on-screen prompts and conversational text, but it substitutes traditional alarms with haptic feedback and uses color-coding for task heaviness.
*   _Novelty_: A proactive "life-navigation assistant" that respects user autonomy and communicates with subtlety.

**[Idea #2]**: Mood-Aware Task Triage
*   _Concept_: The assistant performs simple mood checks to gauge the user's state of mind and curates task suggestions accordingly (deep work, creative tasks, or de-stressing activities).
*   _Novelty_: Manages user energy, not just time, to prevent burnout and align work with natural rhythms.

**[Idea #3]**: Proactive Social Scheduling
*   _Concept_: The assistant actively helps maintain the user's social and professional network by cross-referencing calendar gaps with relationship-based tasks and proactively suggesting scheduling them.
*   _Novelty_: Elevates social tasks from a simple "to-do" to a scheduled, prioritized activity.

**[Idea #4]**: Gamified Goal Decomposition (Without Progress Bars)
*   _Concept_: The assistant breaks large goals into smaller, scheduled tasks. Gamification focuses on points, levels, and satisfying checklist completions to provide a psychological boost without visual progress bars.
*   _Novelty_: Automates the planning and scheduling phase of goal-setting and adds a layer of minimalist, positive reinforcement.

**[Idea #5]**: Intelligent Curation via Smart Tags
*   _Concept_: The user can quickly capture information using simple tags like `@movie`. The assistant automatically identifies, curates, and enriches this information with relevant metadata (summaries, links, etc.), creating visual, organized collections.
*   _Novelty_: Combines the ease of plain-text capture with the power of a structured database without friction.

**[Idea #6]**: The Hub-and-Spoke Knowledge Workflow
*   _Concept_: The assistant acts as a temporary hub for captured information, automatically syncing plain text to a knowledge base like Obsidian, and creating reminders for the user to manually add richer content to other specialized apps.
*   _Novelty_: Defines the assistant as an intelligent data-flow manager that respects the user's tool ecosystem.

**[Idea #7]**: The Daily Debrief Dashboard
*   _Concept_: A visual daily review dashboard showing mood logs, task status (completed/postponed), and new items created. It concludes with a prompt for an evening review and a nudge to plan the next day.
*   _Novelty_: An actionable end-of-day ritual that bridges today's accomplishments with tomorrow's plan.

**[Idea #8]**: Sticky Note Ingestion
*   _Concept_: The user can take a photo of a single sticky note, which the assistant instantly digitizes using OCR. This is treated as a high-priority, versatile capture method for tasks, ideas, or notes.
*   _Novelty_: A powerful, tactile workflow that bridges the physical and digital worlds for high-value thoughts.

**[Idea #9]**: Finch-Style Mood-Driven Scheduling
*   _Concept_: When a low or stressed mood is detected, the assistant actively offers to schedule mood-boosting activities like walks, breathing exercises, or journaling sessions.
*   _Novelty_: The assistant becomes a mental well-being partner, actively scheduling restorative activities.

**[Idea #10]**: Gamified Recurring Habit Tracker
*   _Concept_: Combines the "recurring task" feature with gamification (streaks, points) to create an engaging habit tracker.
*   _Novelty_: Transforms a mundane feature into a core motivational engine for building positive habits.

**[Idea #11]**: The Pomodoro Power-Up
*   _Concept_: Integrates the Pomodoro timer with deep work tasks. During breaks, it proactively suggests completing saved articles or flash tasks.
*   _Novelty_: Makes the entire Pomodoro cycle productive and intentional.

**[Idea #12]**: The Quest Log Dashboard
*   _Concept_: Redesigns the dashboard as a video game "Quest Log" with "Main Quests" (Top 3 tasks), a leveling-up system, and a "Journey Timeline" for an overall review.
*   _Novelty_: Shifts the user experience from a passive list to an active, engaging "game" of productivity.

**[Idea #13]**: The Personal Performance Consultant
*   _Concept_: After collecting enough data from the Daily Debrief, the assistant analyzes trends to offer personalized, data-driven insights on the user's work style, friction points, and well-being.
*   _Novelty_: Interprets raw data to offer personalized, actionable coaching for self-improvement.

### Action Plans (High-Impact)

**Idea #1: The Jarvis Co-Pilot (Enhanced Communication)**
*   **Why This Matters:** This is the foundational identity of the entire product. Getting the persona and its subtle communication right will define the user's relationship with the assistant.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Define Persona Attributes:** Write a 1-page brief detailing the "Jarvis" personality: tone of voice, vocabulary, rules of engagement (e.g., never initiates conversation unless critical).
    2.  **Prototype UI/UX for Communication:** Create mockups for the on-screen prompts, the color-coding system for task heaviness, and the haptic feedback patterns.
    3.  **User-Test the Persona:** Create 3-5 sample conversational interactions and get feedback from target users (students/young professionals) on whether the tone feels helpful, not controlling.
*   **Resources Needed:** UX/UI design tools (Figma, Sketch), user testing group.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Making the AI feel "intelligent" without being "creepy" is a fine line. Haptic feedback may require specific mobile OS permissions and development.
*   **Success Metrics:** In user tests, 90%+ of users describe the persona as "helpful," "calm," or "supportive."

---

**Idea #2: Mood-Aware Task Triage**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature directly addresses user well-being and energy management, a key differentiator from purely task-based systems.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Design the Mood Input UI:** Create a simple, low-friction UI for the emoji-based mood check-in. Decide on the frequency and triggers for this check-in.
    2.  **Map Moods to Task Categories:** Define the logic. For example: Mood 1-2 (Low) -> suggest de-stressing activities; Mood 3 (Neutral) -> suggest "flash tasks"; Mood 4-5 (High) -> protect for "deep work."
    3.  **Create a "De-Stressing Library":** Curate a small, MVP list of de-stressing activities the assistant can suggest (e.g., link to a 5-minute meditation, suggest a walk, etc.).
*   **Resources Needed:** List of curated activities, UI design tools.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Users may not consistently log their mood; the system needs to be useful even with sporadic data.
*   **Success Metrics:** A user who logs a "low" mood engages with a suggested de-stressing activity 50%+ of the time.

---

**Idea #3: Proactive Social Scheduling**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature demonstrates true "personal assistant" capabilities by managing an area of life that often gets neglected.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Prototype the Calendar/Task Integration:** Design the logic for how the assistant scans the calendar for free slots and cross-references them with tasks that have people's names in them.
    2.  **Draft the Suggestion Language:** Write the specific prompts the assistant will use (e.g., "I see you have a free hour on Friday. You have a task to 'Catch up with Sarah.' Shall I schedule a call?").
    3.  **Define the "Contact" object:** How does the system know about "Sarah"? Does it pull from the phone's contacts? Is there an internal CRM? Define the minimum viable data structure.
*   **Resources Needed:** Calendar API access (e.g., Google Calendar API), contacts integration plan.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Privacy concerns around accessing contacts and calendars must be addressed upfront. The logic needs to be smart enough not to suggest meetings at inappropriate times.
*   **Success Metrics:** The system successfully identifies and suggests a valid social scheduling opportunity within the first week of use for a new user.

---

### Action Plans (Most Innovative)

**Idea #5: Intelligent Curation via Smart Tags**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature defines the primary, frictionless method for getting information *into* your system and makes the assistant immediately useful for organization.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Define the Tagging Syntax:** Finalize the syntax (e.g., `@tag: content` or `content @tag`). Define the initial set of supported tags (`@movie`, `@book`, `@rabbit-hole`, `@person`).
    2.  **Prototype the Parser:** Create a simple function that can take a string of text and correctly extract the tag and the content.
    3.  **Identify APIs for Enrichment:** Research and select APIs for enriching the content (e.g., IMDb/OMDb for movies, Google Books API for books, Wikipedia for general concepts). Create a developer account and get API keys.
*   **Resources Needed:** API documentation for selected services, a text parsing library.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** API rate limits or costs could be a factor. The parser needs to be robust enough to handle messy, real-world user input.
*   **Success Metrics:** The system can correctly parse and enrich 95% of tagged inputs from a test set.

---

**Idea #8: Sticky Note Ingestion**
*   **Why This Matters:** This is a "wow" feature that bridges the physical and digital divide, respecting that deep thinking often happens on paper.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Research OCR Services:** Evaluate mobile-friendly Optical Character Recognition (OCR) services (e.g., Google's ML Kit, Apple's Vision framework, or third-party APIs). Select one for an initial prototype.
    2.  **Build a Basic Camera UI:** Create a simple mobile view that allows the user to open a camera and take a picture.
    3.  **Proof of Concept:** Connect the camera UI to the chosen OCR service and test if it can accurately convert text from a picture of a handwritten sticky note into a digital string. Focus on accuracy above all else for now.
*   **Resources Needed:** Mobile development environment (iOS/Android), selected OCR service/library.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Handwriting recognition is notoriously difficult and can vary wildly between users. Lighting conditions will affect OCR accuracy.
*   **Success Metrics:** The prototype can achieve >90% character accuracy on your own handwriting in good lighting.

### Action Plans (High-Impact)

**Idea #1: The Jarvis Co-Pilot (Enhanced Communication)**
*   **Why This Matters:** This is the foundational identity of the entire product. Getting the persona and its subtle communication right will define the user's relationship with the assistant.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Define Persona Attributes:** Write a 1-page brief detailing the "Jarvis" personality: tone of voice, vocabulary, rules of engagement (e.g., never initiates conversation unless critical).
    2.  **Prototype UI/UX for Communication:** Create mockups for the on-screen prompts, the color-coding system for task heaviness, and the haptic feedback patterns.
    3.  **User-Test the Persona:** Create 3-5 sample conversational interactions and get feedback from target users (students/young professionals) on whether the tone feels helpful, not controlling.
*   **Resources Needed:** UX/UI design tools (Figma, Sketch), user testing group.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Making the AI feel "intelligent" without being "creepy" is a fine line. Haptic feedback may require specific mobile OS permissions and development.
*   **Success Metrics:** In user tests, 90%+ of users describe the persona as "helpful," "calm," or "supportive."

---

**Idea #2: Mood-Aware Task Triage**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature directly addresses user well-being and energy management, a key differentiator from purely task-based systems.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Design the Mood Input UI:** Create a simple, low-friction UI for the emoji-based mood check-in. Decide on the frequency and triggers for this check-in.
    2.  **Map Moods to Task Categories:** Define the logic. For example: Mood 1-2 (Low) -> suggest de-stressing activities; Mood 3 (Neutral) -> suggest "flash tasks"; Mood 4-5 (High) -> protect for "deep work."
    3.  **Create a "De-Stressing Library":** Curate a small, MVP list of de-stressing activities the assistant can suggest (e.g., link to a 5-minute meditation, suggest a walk, etc.).
*   **Resources Needed:** List of curated activities, UI design tools.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Users may not consistently log their mood; the system needs to be useful even with sporadic data.
*   **Success Metrics:** A user who logs a "low" mood engages with a suggested de-stressing activity 50%+ of the time.

---

**Idea #3: Proactive Social Scheduling**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature demonstrates true "personal assistant" capabilities by managing an area of life that often gets neglected.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Prototype the Calendar/Task Integration:** Design the logic for how the assistant scans the calendar for free slots and cross-references them with tasks that have people's names in them.
    2.  **Draft the Suggestion Language:** Write the specific prompts the assistant will use (e.g., "I see you have a free hour on Friday. You have a task to 'Catch up with Sarah.' Shall I schedule a call?").
    3.  **Define the "Contact" object:** How does the system know about "Sarah"? Does it pull from the phone's contacts? Is there an internal CRM? Define the minimum viable data structure.
*   **Resources Needed:** Calendar API access (e.g., Google Calendar API), contacts integration plan.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Privacy concerns around accessing contacts and calendars must be addressed upfront. The logic needs to be smart enough not to suggest meetings at inappropriate times.
*   **Success Metrics:** The system successfully identifies and suggests a valid social scheduling opportunity within the first week of use for a new user.

---

### Action Plans (Most Innovative)

**Idea #5: Intelligent Curation via Smart Tags**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature defines the primary, frictionless method for getting information *into* your system and makes the assistant immediately useful for organization.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Define the Tagging Syntax:** Finalize the syntax (e.g., `@tag: content` or `content @tag`). Define the initial set of supported tags (`@movie`, `@book`, `@rabbit-hole`, `@person`).
    2.  **Prototype the Parser:** Create a simple function that can take a string of text and correctly extract the tag and the content.
    3.  **Identify APIs for Enrichment:** Research and select APIs for enriching the content (e.g., IMDb/OMDb for movies, Google Books API for books, Wikipedia for general concepts). Create a developer account and get API keys.
*   **Resources Needed:** API documentation for selected services, a text parsing library.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** API rate limits or costs could be a factor. The parser needs to be robust enough to handle messy, real-world user input.
*   **Success Metrics:** The system can correctly parse and enrich 95% of tagged inputs from a test set.

---

**Idea #8: Sticky Note Ingestion**
*   **Why This Matters:** This is a "wow" feature that bridges the physical and digital divide, respecting that deep thinking often happens on paper.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Research OCR Services:** Evaluate mobile-friendly Optical Character Recognition (OCR) services (e.g., Google's ML Kit, Apple's Vision framework, or third-party APIs). Select one for an initial prototype.
    2.  **Build a Basic Camera UI:** Create a simple mobile view that allows the user to open a camera and take a picture.
    3.  **Proof of Concept:** Connect the camera UI to the chosen OCR service and test if it can accurately convert text from a picture of a handwritten sticky note into a digital string. Focus on accuracy above all else for now.
*   **Resources Needed:** Mobile development environment (iOS/Android), selected OCR service/library.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Handwriting recognition is notoriously difficult and can vary wildly between users. Lighting conditions will affect OCR accuracy.
*   **Success Metrics:** The prototype can achieve >90% character accuracy on your own handwriting in good lighting.

---

### Action Plans (Other Ideas)

*   **[Idea #6] The Hub-and-Spoke Knowledge Workflow:**
    *   **Next Step:** Investigate the APIs for your primary knowledge base (e.g., Obsidian, Capacities) to understand the feasibility and requirements for automated data syncing.
*   **[Idea #7] The Daily Debrief Dashboard:**
    *   **Next Step:** Design a low-fidelity wireframe of the dashboard layout, showing where the mood log, task summary, and "plan for tomorrow" nudge would be placed.
*   **[Idea #9] Finch-Style Mood-Driven Scheduling:**
    *   **Next Step:** This is a natural extension of Idea #2. Once mood-tracking is in place, the first step is to prototype the logic that connects a "low" mood to a suggestion from the "De-Stressing Library."
*   **[Idea #10] Gamified Recurring Habit Tracker:**
    *   **Next Step:** Design the data model for a "habit." How is it different from a standard recurring task? Define attributes like "streak count" and "last completed date."
*   **[Idea #11] The Pomodoro Power-Up:**
    *   **Next Step:** Create a basic UI for a Pomodoro timer. The first integration is to automatically start it when a "deep work" task is initiated.
*   **[Idea #12] The Quest Log Dashboard:**
    *   **Next Step:** This is a major UI theme. The first step is to create a mood board or collect screenshots from games that capture the desired aesthetic for the dashboard.
*   **[Idea #13] The Personal Performance Consultant:**
    *   **Next Step:** Define the key metrics to track for an MVP version. For example, start by simply tracking "Task Completion Rate by Day of the Week" and "Mood Correlation with Completed Tasks."

*   **Why This Matters:** This feature directly addresses user well-being and energy management, a key differentiator from purely task-based systems.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Design the Mood Input UI:** Create a simple, low-friction UI for the emoji-based mood check-in. Decide on the frequency and triggers for this check-in.
    2.  **Map Moods to Task Categories:** Define the logic. For example: Mood 1-2 (Low) -> suggest de-stressing activities; Mood 3 (Neutral) -> suggest "flash tasks"; Mood 4-5 (High) -> protect for "deep work."
    3.  **Create a "De-Stressing Library":** Curate a small, MVP list of de-stressing activities the assistant can suggest (e.g., link to a 5-minute meditation, suggest a walk, etc.).
*   **Resources Needed:** List of curated activities, UI design tools.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Users may not consistently log their mood; the system needs to be useful even with sporadic data.
*   **Success Metrics:** A user who logs a "low" mood engages with a suggested de-stressing activity 50%+ of the time.

**Idea #3: Proactive Social Scheduling**
*   **Why This Matters:** This feature demonstrates true "personal assistant" capabilities by managing an area of life that often gets neglected.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Prototype the Calendar/Task Integration:** Design the logic for how the assistant scans the calendar for free slots and cross-references them with tasks that have people's names in them.
    2.  **Draft the Suggestion Language:** Write the specific prompts the assistant will use (e.g., "I see you have a free hour on Friday. You have a task to 'Catch up with Sarah.' Shall I schedule a call?").
    3.  **Define the "Contact" object:** How does the system know about "Sarah"? Does it pull from the phone's contacts? Is there an internal CRM? Define the minimum viable data structure.
*   **Resources Needed:** Calendar API access (e.g., Google Calendar API), contacts integration plan.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Privacy concerns around accessing contacts and calendars must be addressed upfront. The logic needs to be smart enough not to suggest meetings at inappropriate times.
*   **Success Metrics:** The system successfully identifies and suggests a valid social scheduling opportunity within the first week of use for a new user.

*   **Potential Obstacles:** API rate limits or costs could be a factor. The parser needs to be robust enough to handle messy, real-world user input.
*   **Success Metrics:** The system can correctly parse and enrich 95% of tagged inputs from a test set.

**Idea #8: Sticky Note Ingestion**
*   **Why This Matters:** This is a "wow" feature that bridges the physical and digital divide, respecting that deep thinking often happens on paper.
*   **Immediate Next Steps:**
    1.  **Research OCR Services:** Evaluate mobile-friendly Optical Character Recognition (OCR) services (e.g., Google's ML Kit, Apple's Vision framework, or third-party APIs). Select one for an initial prototype.
    2.  **Build a Basic Camera UI:** Create a simple mobile view that allows the user to open a camera and take a picture.
    3.  **Proof of Concept:** Connect the camera UI to the chosen OCR service and test if it can accurately convert text from a picture of a handwritten sticky note into a digital string. Focus on accuracy above all else for now.
*   **Resources Needed:** Mobile development environment (iOS/Android), selected OCR service/library.
*   **Potential Obstacles:** Handwriting recognition is notoriously difficult and can vary wildly between users. Lighting conditions will affect OCR accuracy.
*   **Success Metrics:** The prototype can achieve >90% character accuracy on your own handwriting in good lighting.
